# Delivery and visual evidence

Use this when presentation mode changes what belongs on slides, or supporting material does not yet let the audience inspect the point. The presentation keeps its source and evidence contract. Product-page copy remains ordinary writing; a product story expressed as slides belongs here.

## Allocate explanation for the actual mode

For live delivery, speech can carry narration and transitions while slides provide material the audience needs to inspect. Keep qualifications that govern a visible claim accessible with it during live delivery as well. For independent reading, include enough context, labels, and explanation to understand the point without absent narration.

Mixed use can be handled by one autonomous deck, pre-reading followed by discussion, or separate versions if the task supports them. A deck used selectively in a meeting needs readable, retrievable sections. Choose from known delivery conditions; do not create an extra deck by default. A live demonstration or working file requires preparation and reliable navigation.

Hypothetical supplied finding: review time decreased within a selected pilot group; general effects and causality are not established. A slide can say “Review time fell in the selected pilot group” and show the group's measurements under their actual conditions. “The rollout reduced review time” would expand scope and assert causality. The pilot limitation belongs with the result; narration about how discussion will proceed can stay in notes.

## Make the title a retrieval promise

Use a supported conclusion when the slide establishes one. Use an accurate topic title to introduce a subject, orient the audience, present reference material, or frame an open question when a conclusion would overstate the evidence. Then ensure the content delivers that promise.

If a slide spills across unrelated topics, narrow its point, regroup material, or move genuinely optional reference content. Intentional recaps and cumulative arguments can need cross-slide relationships. An independently retrieved slide needs local identity and the conditions that preserve its meaning.

## Select what the audience can inspect

Choose the relationship the audience needs to understand, then select a chart, screenshot, diagram, quotation, prominent number, phrase, or selected text that exposes it. Text-only slides can work. A stock image can establish atmosphere but cannot substantiate claims about actual complaints, throughput, or customer results.

For a comparison, use compatible measurements or views and identify the baseline and conditions that affect interpretation. Hypothetical example: to explain a settings-flow change, show before and after screenshots for the same task and identify their respective versions. Point to the changed step. Two unrelated screens do not establish improvement, and a shorter-looking path does not prove measured time savings. A slide that does not make a comparison needs no invented baseline.

Actual excerpts can illustrate reported experiences without establishing how representative they are. Bullets should enumerate comparable items connected to their lead-in; a narrative or developed argument may need prose or a diagram.

## Direct attention with captions and callouts

A callout identifies the decisive region or relation. A caption supplies context the image lacks: environment, date, meaning of a marker, or why a comparison matters. It need not repeat the obvious appearance.

Use enough annotation to support the point without forcing the audience to search through decoration. There is no fixed annotation or image quota. Prefer a useful closeup over a tiny whole-screen image when a small control is the subject; retain surrounding context when needed to identify it.

A closing recap can repeat important results. Distinguish that summary from a conclusion about what follows; an inferred implication needs evidence and appropriate qualification. Keep intentional repetition when it serves a second reading moment.

Check the slide at its intended size and mode when the artifact workflow provides that capability. Contrast, text size, and accessible alternatives need context-specific attention; this reference establishes no universal font size or accessibility guarantee. Missing assets remain missing support in the draft.
