# Revision decisions

Use the relevant section when a clearer revision may change meaning, focus, access, or stance. The requested editing mode and established invariants govern each repair. Diagnose from supplied text and known context; a routine edit need not acquire a new brief or research phase.

## Choose the repair by the phrase's function

First identify whether a phrase carries an assertion, uncertainty, attribution, relationship, voice, or navigation. Then choose deletion, rewording, restructuring, supported specification, or retention. A passive construction, vague adjective, aside, or formula is a prompt to inspect its function, not a defect by itself.

For empty praise, find the asserted property or process before supplying a stronger alternative. If the task supplies no detail, a cleaner sentence or a note naming the missing fact may complete the repair. Keep editorial queries separate from final copy.

Hypothetical neutral-tone revision:

- Before: “Unfortunately, in my personal view, the report is probably incomplete.”
- After: “In my view, the report is probably incomplete.”

This removes an expression of regret when neutral tone is requested, while retaining personal attribution and probability. “The report is incomplete” changes both. Under a brief that values the regret or cautious personal voice, retaining more of the original can be correct. Tone permission does not authorize a new certainty level.

Russian wording such as “В целях осуществления проверки” can often become “Чтобы проверить” when purpose and actor remain the same. This is a syntax example, not a list of English tokens to forbid. Courtesy, humor, deliberate hesitation, and contractual terms can carry necessary meaning.

## Test tempting specificity

The following are original hypothetical cases, not equivalent replacements:

| Supplied wording | Tempting change | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| “Send the form ten days before the visit.” | “Send the form by Friday.” | A relative deadline becomes a calendar deadline; equivalence needs the visit date and counting rule |
| “Available on every calendar day.” | “Available 24 hours a day.” | Daily availability becomes continuous availability |
| “At least 60 entries.” | “About 60 entries.” | A lower bound becomes an approximation |
| “Inspection may have delayed the package.” | “Inspection delayed the package.” | A possible cause becomes established |
| “Eligible if both conditions apply.” | “Choose either condition.” | A conjunction becomes an alternative |
| “I believe the policy needs revision.” | “The policy needs revision.” | A personal position becomes unqualified |
| “We apologize for the delay.” | “We will prevent another delay.” | An apology becomes a commitment |

Specific actors, dates, restoration windows, mechanisms, and performance outcomes need support. Use verified context within scope, keep the less specific supported assertion, or identify the missing input separately. A request must not become an order, a manager's remark company policy, or a product description an unsupported technical classification.

## Preserve the passage's focus and information order

Read the paragraph before changing its subject. An explicit person and active verb are useful when responsibility matters; passive, state, or impersonal wording may preserve the topic better.

Hypothetical example: “The north wing was reopened on Monday. Its reading rooms now share one entrance.” Adding “The facilities team reopened…” shifts attention to an actor and requires evidence identifying that actor. If the passage describes the building, the original may work better.

When qualifiers bury an event, change information arrival without resolving ambiguous cause or timing. Hypothetical supplied facts: the team surveyed the loading entrance last month, held two discussions that month, then moved the delivery point. The cause of the move is not supplied.

- Before: “After last month's survey of the loading entrance and two discussions held that month, the team moved the delivery point.”
- After: “The team moved the delivery point. The move followed last month's survey of the loading entrance and two discussions held that month.”

The revision moves the event earlier while preserving sequence and timing. Adding “because the entrance was blocked” would invent a cause. When the original attachment is ambiguous, preserve or flag it; fluent prose cannot decide the facts.

Split at changes in thought, not a word or comma threshold. Enriching a sentence with supported dates, services, and conditions can require regrouping rather than one longer sentence. Preserve distinct items and short necessary qualifications even when they cannot fill separate paragraphs.

## Accepted-draft access and continuity

When restructuring is authorized and specialist substance is accepted, change access before paraphrasing: group related material, put the current answer before background where appropriate, and use meaningful headings or an opening summary. Keep stable wording and detail where they work. Substantive technical, execution, and presentation revisions retain their artifact owner.

Read paragraph openings in sequence for topic cues, time jumps, and missing connections. Repair the smallest authorized unit; a missing transition may need one clause. If bold fragments become disconnected keywords, move the proposition into an opening or heading while retaining useful emphasis.

Read likely entry points without their parents. A heading or action must retain its subject, polarity, and governing conditions. For example, “Annual-plan limits” can preserve an identity lost in a bare “Limits” subsection. Keep repeated warnings, local prerequisites, summary/body overlap, and a long letter's repeated ask when they serve separate reading moments. A request or “no action needed” line is useful only when its absence leaves uncertainty.

Before converting prose to bullets, identify what is enumerated. Check semantic levels, repeated-field order, and whether the lead-in applies to every item. Keep common conditions accessible and distinguish cumulative requirements from alternatives. Use a table for repeated attributes; let a developed argument remain prose or a section.

After cuts and splits, read continuously again. Restore a needed connective or join related sentences. Do not add “therefore,” “because,” or an explicit actor unless the relationship is established. Preserve chronology when sequence is evidence.

## Official replies and notices

Identify the function before simplifying. A reply can put the supported answer before its grounds, followed by a verified action available to the reader. An evidentiary record may require identifiers and their relationships in a particular place.

Retain who did what, to whom, under which conditions, with what authority and certainty. Preserve all/any logic and identifier attachment. A program is not interchangeable with one event inside it; an everyday synonym can change a legal referent or technical category.

Provide routes, forms, deadlines, and contacts when the task supplies them and they resolve the next step. An incomplete notice can become clearer without inventing a restoration estimate. Suggesting a human action in the text does not authorize executing it.

## Tone and stance

When tone repair is requested, inspect the proposition and relationship before surface markers: accusation, ranking the reader below the author, invented intimacy, or concealed commercial purpose. Removing an emoji does not repair these meanings.

Hypothetical supplied wording: “As you should already know, we cannot review an incomplete submission.” For a requested courteous tone, “We need the complete submission before review can begin” removes the reprimand and keeps the condition. Do not replace “complete submission” with a guessed missing file or invent a prior conversation. If completion requires several items, naming only one must not imply that it is sufficient.

A requested tone edit can remove an unintended accusation without another permission ritual. A pure copyedit should not silently reverse an intended accusation or commercial position. Preserve intentional warmth, enthusiasm, irony, firmness, formality, or a requested persona when it serves the brief. Fictional voice needs consistent characterization, not a replacement with a real speaker or a repeated verbal tic.
