# Product Leadership And Operating-Model Decisions

Related: [business thinking](01-business-thinking.md), [team flow](08-team-topologies-fast-flow.md), [risk](09-risk-and-second-order-effects.md), [product operating model](../../product-design/references/02-product-operating-model.md)

Use the leadership sections for requested role, capability or succession advice; use the intervention and trial sections for requested changes to product work. This reference does not turn product design or implementation into a transformation program. Reuse established decisions and authorization; proposing a change does not authorize recruiting participants, changing roles or rollout.

## Diagnose The Leadership Gap

Start with the missing capability and its consequence. Strong individual product judgment, developing other people, providing direction and coordinating delivery are different contributions. Evidence of one does not establish the others.

- For product leadership, examine who provides direction, understands the business, develops judgment in others and can sustain those contributions through succession. Assess complementarity with existing leadership, including whether a departing person was the actual source of vision.
- For engineering leadership, examine the relevant gap across people development, business decisions, reliable delivery and debt, architecture, discovery participation and representation to other functions. Use observed work and system constraints rather than a universal performance scorecard.
- For cross-team coherence, identify responsibility for conflicting business rules, end-to-end experience and technology choices. Check accessibility and continuity of that knowledge. Documentation and shared artifacts support continuity alongside capable people and working relationships.

Compare ways to cover the gap before prescribing a hire or management layer. An experienced individual contributor can deepen judgment without managing people; a functional manager can focus on development; a group product role can combine product work with coaching related PMs if capacity permits. Titles do not settle accountability or approval rights. Do not infer capability from hours worked, education, enthusiasm or fixed staffing ratios.

If a successful PM becomes a manager and teams still lack useful feedback, inspect coaching skill and protected time before concluding that more PMs are needed. If vision disappeared with a founder's departure, preserving the replacement's title will not restore the contribution. When leaders import practices from another company, examine which customer, scale and dependency assumptions transfer; this is an onboarding question, not a hiring filter.

## Protect Attention By Removing Impediments

If product leaders lose time to cross-team and functional blockers, locate the work and its cost before adding a delivery/project role. Such a role is useful when it removes impediments and coordinates dependencies, capacity and consequential dates. Planning and status artifacts help when they serve that purpose; their existence is neither proof of value nor an automatic anti-pattern.

Keep solution judgment and team commitments with the appropriate product/engineering decision makers. A coordinator does not inherit product authority by maintaining the plan. Evaluate whether the role unblocks work or adds a reporting layer using observed coordination burden, not a team-count threshold. For boundary and interaction problems, return to [team flow](08-team-topologies-fast-flow.md).

## Choose The Intervention For The Problem

For an explicit request to improve product work, distinguish a capability gap from constraints on action:

- Weak discovery may involve poor access to relevant users, unclear direction, limited ability to compare solutions, or lack of room to improve the product.
- Slow delivery may involve debt, architecture, handoffs, missing automation, late specialist involvement or priority churn.
- A team can know how to discover yet lack authority to choose a solution; training alone cannot supply that authority. Autonomy alone does not teach an unfamiliar method.

Inspect the bottleneck before recommending a sprint, coaching, role change or operating-model trial. For a bounded difficult learning question or discovery coaching, use [discovery guidance](../../product-design/references/03-discovery-and-opportunity-solution-trees.md). Assess the coach's demonstrated capability for the gap; delivery experience does not automatically cover discovery, business-model or go-to-market work.

Preserve maintenance that protects the ability to improve. Debt work can crowd out new learning, while neglected architecture can also slow delivery. Use [SPACE](06-developer-productivity-space.md) for productivity and well-being evidence rather than measuring innovation or execution through activity alone.

## Trial An Operating Change

When an organizational trial is in scope, propose a limited setting with willing participants, a meaningful problem, enough authority to try a different way of solving it, and close product/design/engineering work. Identify dependencies on teams retaining the existing process and reduce avoidable friction while preserving legitimate shared controls.

State what should improve, what support the trial receives, available comparison or prior evidence, and what would lead to continuation, adjustment or abandonment. Observe learning and relevant business/product effects over a useful horizon. A willing team receiving extra support is selected; comparisons with prior work or other teams are informative but do not establish a controlled causal effect. Lagging outcomes can remain uncertain even when the trial operates well.

Before recommending expansion, ask which conditions made the trial viable and whether they exist elsewhere. Trial success informs applicability; it is not automatic approval for rollout. Existing external commitments remain until properly changed, and real-user/data exposure retains the relevant protections.

## Share Learning And Interpret Accountability

When significant learning remains siloed, offer a concise account of what was tried, what worked or failed, what remains uncertain and what changes next. Include negative results and invite useful explanations of surprises. Use an existing channel where possible; a new recurring meeting is not a prerequisite.

Distinguish freedom to investigate uncertain ideas from resources and authority to fulfill accepted commitments. Leaders can prioritize problems while teams choose how to solve them. A negative experiment need not imply poor execution, and a missed commitment needs its context: resources, dependencies, decisions and response to emerging evidence. Do not infer a mandate for punishment, chronic stress or a numerical culture score.

## Source And Limits

This is applied decision guidance from Marty Cagan's *Вдохновленные*, МИФ, 2020, ISBN 9785001464310: leadership and roles, transformation and culture. It supplements the separately sourced Team Topologies, SPACE and other linked guidance. The source offers practitioner arguments and selected company stories, not controlled organizational comparisons or transferable effect estimates. It does not establish required staffing spans, working hours, reorganization cadence, a universally effective coaching background or guaranteed transformation outcomes.
