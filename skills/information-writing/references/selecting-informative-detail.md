# Selecting informative detail

Use this when a vague claim invites more facts, or concrete wording still does not help the reader. Cutting, explaining, and obtaining evidence are different repairs.

## Diagnose the missing value

| Test | Symptom | Useful repair |
|---|---|---|
| Supported? | A number, benefit, experience, or cause has no basis in the available material | Retain the supported assertion, qualify it, or identify the missing fact; obtain evidence only within the task's evidence contract |
| Interpretable? | A true credential, measurement, or process name has no clear meaning for this reader | Explain its relevant consequence or comparison, preserving the conditions |
| Relevant? | An understandable fact does not answer the reader's question | Omit it, move it to secondary detail, or select a better supported fact |

Specificity is not proof. A count can be accurate while supporting a different proposition from the one being argued.

Hypothetical example: a music school states that it has 18 teaching rooms. A parent choosing after-school lessons may need location, session times, and age eligibility instead. The room count could help explain capacity, but establishes neither available places nor convenient scheduling. Use actual schedule information if supplied; do not infer it from capacity.

## Choose the smallest useful support

A property says what something does; a mechanism explains how; a bounded situation makes the consequence recognizable; a comparison interprets unfamiliar scale; a demonstration lets the reader inspect behavior or an output. Choose what resolves the gap. These are alternatives or complements, not a ladder every claim must climb.

Hypothetical supplied fact: “Switching the reading lamp on restores its last brightness setting.” A useful explanation is, “When you switch it on again, it uses your previous brightness setting.” “Wake without disturbing anyone” adds an outcome the fact does not establish. Remembering a setting alone would not establish that the lamp applies it automatically; that behavior must also be supplied.

A screenshot can show where a control is but cannot establish the time it saves. A lifestyle photograph can establish atmosphere without demonstrating durability. If a visual would help but is absent, specify what it needs to show rather than describe it as inspected or available. A real output can demonstrate a particular capability without proving typical performance.

## Keep meaning when making wording concrete

“At least 40” is a lower bound; “about 40” is an approximation. Rounding is useful only when exactness is immaterial and the kind of claim stays intact. A warranty period is a commitment under its terms, not demonstrated service life. Explaining a credential must not silently expand its scope or guarantee compatibility.

Keep useful technical terms and define their relevant meaning. A simpler synonym can change a category, obligation, or uncertainty. An unknown actor may itself be the point. A formula such as “we will consider your request” can conceal several intentions; a synonym cannot establish whether a review, delay, or refusal is intended.

Warmth, praise, identity, and ritual language may serve a relationship or expressive brief. Keep them when they perform that job. If “individual approach” is meant to describe a service, look for the actual process. Replacing it with “tailored solutions” adds no information. When the process is unknown, useful cuts or a note naming the missing input can complete the authorized work without inventing a service commitment or blocking unrelated edits.
