# Reader preparation and consumption

Use this when a broad reader mode does not settle depth, emphasis, or the route through an artifact. Use available context and demonstrated capabilities. This is optional diagnostic support, not a questionnaire for every writing or editing task.

## Describe preparation through capabilities

Ask what readers can already recognize or do that the next explanation depends on. Expertise is local: a skilled programmer can be new to a language, and a manager can know the business while needing a measurement-method definition.

Hypothetical contrast: a first command-line setup guide may need where to enter a command and how to recognize success. Experienced developers moving to a new tool may need its installation differences and failure conditions. A maintenance reference can assume setup and begin with the command contract. These are different selections of content, not simply shorter sentences for one group.

Watch for drift toward another imagined audience. A beginner guide can accumulate specialist debates added to impress experts; an expert reference can become burdened with repeated tutorials. Keep the main path useful to the intended reader and put useful alternate depth in labeled sections when the artifact permits it. Keep terms the intended reader needs to learn.

The author's familiarity with a topic does not establish the reader's exposure or recollection. A previous explanation may need a concise reminder, a changed version, or a new access path. Repeat when it serves the present reader, without arbitrary duplication or copying another person's text.

## Compare questions before splitting audiences

Different job titles do not necessarily require different texts. Compare actual questions. Where answers overlap, one main path can serve them. Where evidence or next actions conflict, use labeled entry paths or separate artifacts within the requested scope.

Hypothetical example: a workshop page serves participants asking about prerequisites and a coordinator booking several places. Date, location, and syllabus can be shared; prerequisite guidance and group-booking instructions can be labeled separately. Two complete pages are optional.

A required API reference or runbook can retain one authoritative contract with labeled explanations for different readers. Do not omit correctness-critical conditions because only some readers need them. The artifact owner retains its structure and requirements; adaptation changes access within that contract.

## Clarify likely expectation mismatches

An opening can identify prerequisites, scope, practical purpose, unusual sequence, or a relevant author stake. Include only what prevents a plausible misunderstanding. Avoid a ceremonial preamble when the answer is already clear.

Hypothetical scope line: “This guide starts after you have exported the records; it covers checking the export, not connecting a new source.” Use this only when actual scope and the likely entry point justify it. A sponsor, reviewer relationship, or claimed first-hand experience is a fact and must be true. A tone or clarity request alone does not imply a new audience.

## Fit consumption conditions

Consider whether readers seek the answer or still need a truthful reason to attend. Usefulness can include curiosity and experience; it need not become a sales call to action. Concision alone does not establish that a text will earn attention in a feed or persuade an unwilling audience.

Then consider what the medium allows: rereading, pausing, searching, scanning, viewing a reference, or taking notes. For listening, “use the option in the left column” may need the spoken option name and enough context to identify it. Repeated orientation can help a listener who cannot look back. Keep conditions required for the task even when the medium is brief.

For selective reading, inspect headings and openings independently: local subject, scope, and conditions must remain recoverable. For cumulative explanations, retain connecting context. If actual slide delivery is in scope, the presentation owner handles its format; a listening constraint alone does not make prose a slide artifact.
